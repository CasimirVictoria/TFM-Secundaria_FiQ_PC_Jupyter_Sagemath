# Yes, **integrating computational thinking (CT) generally improves secondary students’ learning in physics, chemistry and broader science**.



**Figure 1:** Overall research consensus on CT and science learning

## Evidence in Physics and Chemistry

- A 6‑week CT intervention using VPython with 12th‑grade physics students produced significantly higher post‑test scores on physics concepts than traditional instruction (M=24.0 vs. 20.3), indicating **better conceptual understanding and visual reasoning** in mechanics topics  (Bufasi et al., 2022).  
- Systematic reviews of CT in physics learning (2019–2024) report that CT‑based modules (e.g., Scratch, Arduino, Visual Python) in topics like electricity, heat, kinematics, energy and work frequently **improve conceptual understanding, critical thinking, collaboration and algorithmic skills** in high‑school students, though technology access and teacher preparation are challenges  (Diraya et al., 2025; Khasanah et al., 2025).  
- A chemistry study integrating **computational chemistry** (Gaussian modeling) in upper‑secondary classes found **rapid gains in students’ confidence** in core chemistry topics and moderate gains in knowledge after modeling bonding, interactions and electron density  (Hrubeš et al., 2024).

## Conditions that Matter

- **Duration:** Interventions of several weeks to 3–12 months show stronger effects  (Yang et al., 2025; Cheng et al., 2022).  
- **Tools & pedagogy:** Modeling‑based, project‑based, and simulation activities using block‑based programming, VPython, Scratch, Arduino and similar tools are most effective  (Yang et al., 2025; Ogegbo & Ramnarain, 2021; Diraya et al., 2025; Bufasi et al., 2022; Khasanah et al., 2025).  
- **Teacher support:** Successful physics/chemistry implementations require teacher training and careful curricular integration  (Ogegbo & Ramnarain, 2021; Li et al., 2020; Diraya et al., 2025; Khasanah et al., 2025).  

## Conclusion

Across secondary and high‑school settings, integrating computational thinking into physics and chemistry—especially via modeling and project‑based activities—**consistently supports better conceptual understanding and key scientific practices**, provided that teachers, tools, and time are adequate.
